# Method and sources

This story uses data from the City of Bologna's TALEA web apps. Here you will find a brief explanation of what we measure and the limitations involved.

## Observation period

The analyses cover thirteen summers, from 2013 to 2025, during June, July and August.

## Air and surface

The opening chart uses the summer average of daily maximum air temperatures: it shows how hot the days become. Surface temperature, recorded by satellites, shows where urban materials accumulate heat. They are two different things: this story is mainly about surfaces.

## What is a climate hotspot?

It is an area that falls within the hottest 10% of Bologna's surfaces in multiple summers. Recurrence indicates how many of the thirteen summers an area fell within that range.

## Shade data

These simulations are based on the city's three-dimensional model and calculated at 15-minute intervals. They show how often streets and green areas remain sheltered during the hottest hours of summer. They describe protection from direct sunlight, not temperature: this is why a heavily shaded street can still remain hot.

## Who is most exposed

Climate vulnerability cannot be read from the thermometer alone: it brings together how much an area heats up, who lives there and how many opportunities it offers to find relief. The 75% cited in the story comes from a published study on Bologna, linked in the text, rather than from an analysis produced by this web app.

## How climate refuges are counted

The 29 places recognised by the municipality, 16 indoor and 13 outdoor, form an official list. The 231 parks and gardens shown alongside them do not: they are green spaces with compatible characteristics, selected through the analysis, and should be read as possibilities, not promises.

## Intervention areas

The two circles on the map mark the areas where the project is working: the Fossolo area and the strip north of the historic centre. They provide indicative context, not the boundaries of individual interventions: they show where, not precisely how far.

## Data limitations

Satellite data and shade simulations are estimates: they describe city-scale trends, not individual days or the heat perceived by a person. They should be read as converging evidence, not exact measurements.
